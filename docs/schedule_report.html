Where we stand today

Four of the six build phases are finished: the testing foundation, images, audio and documents. Photos, music, PDFs and Word files all scrub cleanly, and every result is backed by an automated test rather than a claim. Phase 4 — the camera and video formats — opened last week with iPhone photos.

The three months

  • Add video. MP4 reuses most of the groundwork already laid during the audio phase, so it is the cheapest format left.
  • Begin camera RAW with a survey of what the major camera makers actually put in their files, before any code is written.

31 Oct Feature complete

Every format in, and the tool usable in bulk

  • Camera RAW closes the media phase. The leak that matters is the full-size preview image hidden inside every RAW file, carrying its own copy of the camera and location data.
  • Program files — Windows, Mac and Linux executables, which carry traces of the machine that built them. The rule here is that the program must still run afterwards.
  • Archives and the remaining image types — ZIP, EPUB, TIFF, WebP, SVG, GIF. An archive is cleaned by cleaning everything inside it.
  • Bulk use. Whole folders in one command, and a single published results sheet covering every supported format.

Production readiness

  • Robustness. Make certain that broken or hostile files are refused safely rather than half-processed.

Release candidate

What stays after November

A few limits remain, and they are worth stating once rather than discovering later. Some traces live in the content itself rather than in the metadata — the faint noise a camera sensor leaves in every photograph, or the way a particular program arranged a page — and removing them would damage the file. These are recorded as documented limits rather than left standing as unfinished work.

Figures describing the current state are measured from the project. Dates are the plan; any slippage will be reported against this baseline as it occurs.
